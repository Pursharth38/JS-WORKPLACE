## How to write each section

Use the template in `knowledge-hub-section-template.md` — one copy per section. The short version:

**Answer the question in the first two sentences.** Not a preamble, not "the POSH Act of 2013 was enacted
to…". If the heading is *"Who can file a complaint"*, sentence one says who. Everything else follows. This
is both how people read on phones and how search engines pull an answer snippet.

**300–600 words per section.** Under 200 reads thin. Over 800 and it should probably be two sections —
tell us and we will split it.

**Write it the way you say it in the room.** Plain, direct, second person. *"You must constitute an
Internal Committee at every office with ten or more employees"* — not *"It is incumbent upon the employer
to ensure that an Internal Committee is duly constituted."* Your training voice is the right voice. We are
not looking for legal drafting.

**The practical bit is what makes it yours.** Anyone can restate the section of the Act. What no competitor
can copy is *"the mistake I see most often is companies appointing the HR head as Presiding Officer when
she is not the senior-most woman available"*. Every one of those is a reason someone calls you instead of
reading a PDF. There is a prompt for exactly this in the template — please use it.

**Where a number or timeline exists, state it plainly.** Days, member counts, thresholds, penalty amounts.
These are what people search for and what they screenshot.

**Cite the section of the Act where you rely on it.** Just "§4(2)" or "Section 13" in brackets. We will
format it. This is what makes the page credible to a compliance officer, and it is what a competitor
copying you cannot fake.

## If writing 14 sections is not realistic

Say so now rather than in three weeks. This is the single biggest risk to the timeline and we would much
rather solve it early. Real options, in order of how well they work:

1. **Talk instead of write.** Record a voice note per topic — twenty minutes of you explaining Internal
   Committee constitution the way you would to a client. We transcribe and draft it into the template, you
   edit and approve. Every word still originates with you, and it is roughly a tenth of the effort.
   For most trainers this is the option that actually gets it done.
2. **Reuse what exists.** Training decks, handouts, policy templates, answers you have already written to
   client questions, that long email you sent explaining the inquiry timeline. Send them as they are. We
   will tell you what is usable.
3. **Interview.** We book two 90-minute calls, ask the questions, and draft from your answers. Slowest for
   your calendar, but it works.
4. **Cut the scope.** Ship Wave 1 only and add sections over time. A page that grows is fine — search
   engines reward pages that get updated.

What we cannot do is write it from other POSH websites. That is the one path that is closed, for the two
reasons at the top.
