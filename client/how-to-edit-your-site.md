# How to edit your website

Everything on your site that you would reasonably want to change, you can change
yourself — without asking a developer and without waiting for anyone.

This guide assumes no technical background. If something here does not match what
you see on screen, tell us; the guide is wrong, not you.

---

## Getting in

Go to **yoursite.com/studio** and sign in.

This is your content editor. It is separate from the public website: nothing you
type here appears publicly until you press **Publish**.

> **A note on drafts.** Every change is saved automatically as a draft as you
> type. Drafts are private. The public site only ever shows what you have
> published, so you can leave something half-finished for a week and nobody will
> see it.

---

## The one rule worth remembering

**Publish makes it live.** There is no separate "upload" step and no waiting for a
developer. Changes appear on the public site within about a minute.

If you publish something by mistake, edit it and publish again. Nothing is
permanent except the two things flagged further down.

## What you can change

The left-hand menu is organised the way you think about your business.

### Site settings

Your contact details, in one place. Changing them here changes them **everywhere**
on the site at once — the footer, the contact page, the WhatsApp button.

| Field | Where it shows up |
|---|---|
| Contact email | Footer, contact page, and where enquiry notifications are sent |
| Phone | Contact page and footer |
| WhatsApp number | The floating green button on every page |
| WhatsApp pre-filled message | What is already typed when someone taps that button |
| Address | Footer and contact page |
| Announcement bar | A thin strip across the very top of every page. Leave it switched off unless you have something specific to say |

> **Where your enquiries go.** The "Contact email" field is the address that
> receives every enquiry from the site. If you change it, change it carefully —
> a typo here means enquiries silently stop arriving.

### POSH guide

The long guide at `/posh-act`. This is the most valuable page on your site — it is
what brings people in from Google.

Each section is a separate entry. To add one:

1. Click **POSH guide → Create new**
2. Give it a **heading** — this is what appears on the page and in the table of contents
3. Choose its **group** (Compliance, Internal Committee, Complaints, and so on)
4. Give it a **position** — 1 appears first within its group
5. Write the **content**
6. **Publish**

You can add sections in any order and at any time. The page assembles itself: it
shows the groups in a fixed order, and within each group it uses the position
number you set. Groups with nothing in them simply do not appear, so a
half-finished guide still looks finished.

> ### ⚠️ Never change a "Link ID" after publishing
>
> Each section has a **Link ID** field. It creates a permanent web address like
> `yoursite.com/posh-act#ic-constitution`.
>
> People bookmark these. They paste them into emails and WhatsApp groups. Search
> engines remember them.
>
> If you change a Link ID after publishing, **every link anyone has ever shared to
> that section stops working.** Editing the heading is fine. Editing the Link ID is
> not. If you genuinely need to change one, ask us first — we can redirect the old
> address so nothing breaks.

### IC quick reference

Short reference cards — timelines, penalties, committee composition — as tables.
Same idea as the guide, but built for someone who needs to check one fact quickly
rather than read a page.

Use the **Table** block for these. Add your column headings first, then rows.

### Blog

Posts, and the categories they sit in.

Two fields worth using properly:

- **Excerpt** — the couple of lines shown on the blog list and in Google results.
  Write it as a reason to click, not as a summary.
- **Related Knowledge Hub sections** — link a post to the guide sections it
  relates to. This genuinely helps both pages rank. It takes ten seconds and it is
  the single most valuable optional field on the site.

### Services

What you offer. Each service is one entry with its own page.

The **position** number controls the order they appear in. Lower numbers first.

### Course, chapters and lessons

The paid course. Three levels: a **course** contains **chapters**, and each chapter
contains **lessons**.

> ### ⚠️ Two fields that affect money and access
>
> - **Price** is entered in *paise*, not rupees. ₹4,999 is typed as `499900`.
>   This is the only place the price is set, and it is what the customer is
>   actually charged.
> - **Video length (seconds)** must match the real video. A lesson counts as
>   complete once someone has watched 90% of it — so if this number is wrong,
>   learners either cannot finish the lesson or finish it far too easily.

**Free preview** on a lesson makes it watchable by anyone, without buying. One per
course is a good sample. Any more and there is less reason to pay.

### Assessment questions

Questions for the chapter tests and the final test.

Mark exactly one option as correct — the editor will not let you save otherwise.
The **Topic** field is what a learner is told to revisit if they fail, so keep the
wording consistent between questions ("IC constitution", not sometimes "Internal
Committee setup").

Correct answers are never sent to the learner's browser. Nobody can inspect the
page and read the answer key.

### FAQs and testimonials

FAQs appear on `/faq`, grouped by category and ordered by their position number.

> ### ⚠️ Testimonials need written permission
>
> There is a tickbox: **"Written permission to publish is on file."** A testimonial
> will not save without it, and will not appear on the site.
>
> This is not bureaucracy. Publishing someone's words, name or employer without
> their agreement is a real problem, and it is the kind of thing that is only ever
> noticed after it has gone wrong.
>
> Leave **Organisation** blank unless they have specifically agreed to be named.

## Adding images

Drag and drop into any image field.

Every image asks for **alt text** — a short description of what the image shows.
This is read aloud to people using screen readers and it is read by Google. It is
required, and it takes five seconds: "Jyoti presenting to a group of employees" is
enough.

## Things you cannot change here, and why

| Thing | Why | Who to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Page layout and colours | Consistency across the whole site | Us |
| The privacy, terms and refund pages | They are legal documents; wording changes need care | Us — we will make the change for you |
| Anyone's course progress or certificates | These are records. If they could be edited, a certificate would not mean anything | Us, via a support request |
| Prices already paid | Changing a price does not change past orders, which is correct | — |

## If something looks wrong

1. **Did you press Publish?** Much the most common cause.
2. **Wait a minute and refresh.** Changes take up to about a minute.
3. **Still wrong?** Tell us what you changed and what you expected. Do not undo it
   first — seeing the broken state is usually how we find the cause quickly.

---

## A short checklist for adding a guide section

- [ ] Heading reads like something a person would actually search for
- [ ] Group and position set
- [ ] Link ID looks sensible — and you are happy for it to be permanent
- [ ] Content is your own words
- [ ] Published

---

## One thing to keep in mind

Everything on this site has to be original. Not because we are being strict, but
because copying from another training provider's website has two consequences that
both land on you: Google will not rank near-duplicate content, and the other
provider owns what they wrote.

Your own explanations, in your own words, are the entire asset here. Six sections
written properly are worth more than twenty borrowed.
